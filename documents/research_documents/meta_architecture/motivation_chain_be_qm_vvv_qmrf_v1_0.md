## 5. Historical Narrative

**Stage 1 — Project A:** The research began with the observation that Dignāga–Dharmakīrti's Pramāṇavāda identifies structural concepts (valid cognition, self-certification, temporal discreteness, invalidation) that parallel the quantum measurement problem. A systematic mapping (30 nodes, 39 edges) was developed as comparative philosophy. This exposed a structural gap in Standard QM: its four postulates are silent on the registration architecture of measurement.

**Stage 2 — Project B:** The BE mapping inspired a formal question: can registration-architecture be axiomatized mathematically? K1–K8 were developed as the answer — a formal theory of what constitutes a valid registration event, how registrations order temporally, when they are invalidated, and how registrars relate. BE concepts were preserved as E1–E16 (formal analogues) but K1–K8 stand independent. The φ-map program (Track B) connects K-space to B(H).

**Stage 3 — Project C:** Analyzing K5's invalidation structure suggested a physical question: if prior registrations can invalidate subsequent ones, might there be a suppression effect on outcome probabilities? K5_prospective (conservative extension) and T8 (structural derivation) formalized this intuition. K9_E emerged as the explicit, testable postulate — motivated by K-space but stated independently. K9-S12 is the first dedicated experimental test.
